How do you write lead magnets that go viral on LinkedIn?

In the last 30 days, our lead magnets have generated over 20,000+ comments across all client accounts.

The answer isn't simply "make a better resource"... at least, not at first.

In my experience, lead magnets that explode come down to 3 things:

**1/ The image**

Treat this like a YouTube thumbnail. One big visual idea, a clear proof point, strong contrast, enough curiosity to stop the scroll before anyone reads a word.

Use a screenshot that proves the hook is real. A revenue dashboard, a calendar full of booked calls, a sped-up preview of the resource that shows it exists without giving it away.

If the image doesn't make someone stop mid-scroll before they read a single word, replace it. 95% of lead magnets that underperform are missing a strong image.

**2/ The hook**

The best hooks combine a specific result with an unanswered question.

Be specific about the person, the number, the timeframe, and the mechanism. "I booked 672 demos in 30 days using LinkedIn" will always beat "how to get more leads on LinkedIn."

Write variations around authority, curiosity, and contrarian angles, then pick the one that makes commenting feel obvious.

**3/ The body**

The first 2-3 lines appear in the feed preview before someone clicks see more. Use them.

Give away the pain point immediately in their exact language. Your sharpest observation about what they're struggling with, your most surprising result, the thing that makes them think you wrote this specifically about them.

Then make it clear what's inside the resource and why they can't get it anywhere else.

Once they're reading, structure the post so it's effortless to follow:

> Frame the resource as internal, something you'd normally only give paying clients  
> Add an effort signal: "it took me 27 hours to build this"  
> Add a scarcity or urgency layer  
> Keep the CTA to one word under 6 letters  
> Never put a link in the post body, the comment is the distribution mechanism  
> Before posting, ignore the body for a moment.

Just look at the image, the hook, and the first two lines.

Would you comment if the post wasn't yours?

If the answer isn't an immediate yes, keep working on that first.
