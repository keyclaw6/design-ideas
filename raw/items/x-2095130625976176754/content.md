## Post

My co-founder @ConnorShowler introduced me to topical maps a while back, and I can't unsee it now.

Here is the idea in plain words. Most sites chase keywords one at a time. Find a term, write a post, find another term, write another post. Google reads that as a pile of loose pages.

A topical map flips it. You pick the subject you want to own, split it into hubs and subtopics, decide what each page covers and what it stays out of, and link them so every page has a place. Google reads that as "this site knows the whole subject."

Building this into http://blogr.ai was on the plan since then, and it just shipped. When you connect a site, it starts from what is true today: the keywords you already rank for, who your organic competitors are, the gap between you and them, and how much search demand sits around your subject. All of it comes from a live keyword database, not from a model guessing.

The map is designed from those numbers. Every page comes with its monthly searches, difficulty, intent, your current position, and an opportunity score, so you can see which pages are worth writing first.

Why this beats going keyword by keyword: two pages never fight for the same query, internal links are designed from the start, not patched in at the end, and you pick up the long tail a single post can't reach because the map covers the questions around it. One map, then the articles get written in order.

We also made a free version so you can try it on your own site. Link in the first comment.
