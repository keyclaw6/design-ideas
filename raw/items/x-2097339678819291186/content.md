## Post

@DinScales26

most cold messages get deleted because the sender never learned these:

- rogers, kuiper & kirker
- the self reference effect
- processing depth
- ownership framing

if you send cold messages for a living, save this

50 years of memory research keeps landing on the same finding

information about yourself gets processed deeper and remembered longer than information about anyone else

the brain sorts by relevance to itself before it reads a word

- a message about a category gets skimmed
- a message about the reader gets read
- a message about the reader's own company gets checked
- a message the reader can verify himself gets answered

this is why the prospect decides in under a second whether your message is about him or about people like him

category signals (he's out before line two):

- companies like yours
- businesses in your space
- teams at your stage
- founders who struggle with

these tell him he's one of a thousand and the message was written before he existed

self signals (he keeps reading):

- his company name in the subject
- his city in the first line
- his competitor named
- a result from his exact industry

these tell him the message could only have been sent to him

step 1: put his thing in the first 5 words

not your offer, not your name, his company, his role, or his market

if he has to reach line 3 to find himself, he's gone

step 2: name the gap, not the problem

"visibility" is a problem and every agency talks about it

"you don't come up when someone asks chatgpt for a lawyer in dallas" is a gap, and it's his

the difference is whether he can picture it happening to him

step 3: make the check something he can run himself

this is the most important part

we ran a campaign where the message told law firms a competitor was showing up instead of them in ai search

101 of them replied interested, thats 1 in 4 of every reply

they opened chatgpt, typed their own city, and saw it themselves

by the time they answered, nobody had to convince them of anything

this is where most cold messages fail

they're built around the sender:

- what we do
- who we've worked with
- what makes us different
- how we're the best at it

the prospect ISNT looking for any of that

he's scanning for himself, and if he doesn't find himself in the first line he stops

how self reference should show up in a cold message:

you don't open with who you are

you open with something only true about him

- his outcome plus his company name in the subject
- a result from his exact industry in line one
- a question he can answer about his own situation

then the psychology shifts

he stops asking:

"who is this"

and starts asking:

"how do they know that about me"

that's the moment the message stops being cold

self reference works in cold outbound because:

decision makers process hundreds of messages a week, and relevance to self is the filter that runs before content

the brain gives attention to what's about the self before it decides what to do with it

if your first line could be sent to a thousand people unchanged, it's about a category

if your message describes the prospect's world better than his own website does, he reads to the end

the best cold messages don't introduce the sender

they show the prospect something true about himself that he hadn't checked yet
