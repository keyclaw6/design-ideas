We sent the same 1,000 cold emails twice and tripled the deals on the second run. 

The first run followed the classic playbook and produced 20 replies, 5 calls, and 1 deal. 

The second run produced 3 deals, because that batch had already seen our content 3+ times before the email arrived, and someone who has seen you 3+ times converts 2 to 3x better on the exact same message.

That test is why I stopped believing in "add another channel" as a growth strategy.

After auditing pipelines at 310+ B2B companies, I can tell you the single biggest issue with most GTM motions is the lack of a proper order in choosing and running growth channels. 

Channel two gets added because channel one felt done, channel three because two was slow, and now there are three half-finished motions with no way to tell which one earned which deal.

Here's the way to build your GTM motions so that they actually work:

1. Run one channel until reply rate sits flat for weeks with nothing left to change in the copy. A slow week doesn't count, check the trend across weeks.

2. Then add LinkedIn, posted from the founder's profile. Ours booked over 1,000 meetings in a single year while the company page did basically nothing.

3. When both flatten, wire the two together instead of reaching for a third. Track who's engaging with your content, treat anyone who's seen it 3+ times as warm, and route warm people into the email sequence first.

4. Once the connected system tops out, the next offer is already sitting inside your delivery. The deliverability setup we ran as part of the core service now sells on its own at $60K/mo.

5. Past $250K/mo, stop targeting titles and start targeting what just changed inside the account: funding, hiring spikes in the team you sell into, a new VP inside their first 90 days. That's where the 5 to 11% reply rates on our signal campaigns come from while everyone else sits at 3 to 5%.

And if signal targeting sounds like overkill, one Fortune 500 subsidiary started hammering one of our resource pages one morning, over and over. We sent them a 73-second Loom the same day and it turned into a €200K+ contract.
