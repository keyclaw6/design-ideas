Why AI search prompts, not keywords A Google search query and an AI-assistant prompt are different shapes of the same underlying intent. A keyword is a compressed fragment — "best crm small team". A prompt is the full, natural-language question a person actually asks an assistant like ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Gemini — "what's the best CRM for a 10-person sales team that's easy to set up". Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) is the practice of getting cited inside the assistant's answer to prompts shaped like the second one, not ranked for the first. Research on GEO (Aggarwal et al., KDD 2024) found that optimizing content for generative-engine citation can lift visibility substantially — but the effect size varies by domain and by the type of query being asked. There is no single trick that works for every prompt. That's the argument for testing against a real, varied prompt set specific to your category rather than assuming one tactic covers every query a buyer might ask.
